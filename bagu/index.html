Carry 1.5 L, not 3 L

You will sweat 0.5–0.8 L an hour in Bakun humidity under a pack. Over a 12-hour day that is 6–9 litres — more than anyone can carry. Three litres of water is three kilos and still runs out by early afternoon.

Carry 1.5 L and a squeeze filter instead. Same safety, 1.4 kg lighter, and this route crosses rivers and falls all day. Confirm with your guide where the reliable sources sit before you commit to the light load.

Where to fill

  • Fast-moving water only. Skip standing pools and anything warm.
  • Collect above villages, rice terraces and vegetable gardens. Benguet farms sit right on these watersheds.
  • A filter removes bacteria and protozoa. It does not remove pesticide or fertiliser runoff, so source selection matters more than the filter.
  • Falls and side tributaries beat the main river.

Day 1 / drinking schedule

Night before, in the van
Pre-load

500 ml with an electrolyte tab before you sleep. No alcohol. You cannot fix Day 1 dehydration on Day 1.

60 min before start
Top up, then stop

500 ml with brunch, then stop drinking in bulk so you are not sloshing at the trailhead.

Hours 0–3 · descent and river
Hottest, lowest, sweatiest

600 ml/hr — roughly 150 ml every 15 minutes. One electrolyte tab per litre.

Fill to full at the last good source before the climb starts. Ask the guide which one that is.

Hours 3–7 · assault
Sip on a timer, not on thirst

500–700 ml/hr. Thirst lags dehydration by about 2% body mass. Drink on schedule.

Hours 7–10 · ridge
The trap

Cold air and wind switch off your thirst while you are still sweating hard. This is where people arrive at camp badly behind.

Force 400–500 ml/hr even though you do not want it. Warm it in your mouth if the cold puts you off.

Camp, first 30 min
Refill the tank

750 ml with electrolytes on arrival, another 500 ml with dinner.

Before lights out
Taper

250 ml, then stop an hour before sleep. You want to wake up rested, not at 2 a.m. looking for the CR.

Day 2

On waking
Reset

500 ml with electrolytes before coffee. Check your urine — pale straw means you are ready, dark amber means you start Day 2 in a hole.

On trail
Same cadence

Repeat the Day 1 schedule. Day 2 legs are tired legs, and cramping arrives earlier when sodium is low.

The numbers

A 10–14 hour day at this gain burns somewhere around 4,000–5,500 kcal. You will not eat that, and you should not try. Aim to cover roughly half while moving and accept the deficit.

  • 200–300 kcal per hour on the move
  • 40–60 g carbs per hour — this is the number that actually matters
  • ~500 mg sodium per hour in this heat
  • Eat every 45–60 minutes, on a timer, from hour one

The single most common mistake on a hike this long is not eating in the first three hours because you feel fine. By the time you feel bad, you are four hours behind and cannot catch up while climbing.

Day 1

Hosted brunch, Bakun
Load carbs, go easy on grease

Rice and whatever protein is served. Skip anything heavily fried — fat sits in your stomach for hours and you are about to descend into heat.

Start + 45 min
First snack, whether you want it or not

Something simple and sweet. This one is habit-forming for the rest of the day.

Every 45–60 min after
Alternate sweet and salty

Sweet then salty keeps flavour fatigue away — it is the reason people stop eating on long days. Rotate: gel, then crackers, then trail mix, then chocnut.

~Hour 5
Packed lunch, half of it

Eat half, walk 30 minutes, eat the rest. A full stomach on a steep assault diverts blood you need in your legs.

Final 2 hours
Fast sugar only

Gel or candy. Your gut is tired and will not process anything complicated.

Camp, first 30 min
Recovery window

Carbs plus protein while your muscles are still primed to absorb. Hosted dinner covers this — eat properly even if you feel too wrecked to be hungry. Day 2 is decided here.

Before sleep
Small top-up

A bar or a few crackers. You are still repairing overnight at altitude and cold.

Day 2

Wake
Coffee and carbs

Oatmeal sachet and your 3-in-1. Something in you before the hosted brunch.

On trail
Same 45–60 min cadence

Appetite drops on day two. Set a phone alarm if you have to.

All day
Reserve — do not touch

Two gels and one bar stay sealed and unopened until you are genuinely in trouble or the descent runs past dark. Circuits run long.

Notes

  • Chocnut melts into paste by noon. Bag it separately or accept the mess.
  • Bananas and kakanin are excellent and heavy. One piece, not four.
  • Repack everything out of boxes into ziplocks — saves weight and gives you your trash bags.
  • Nothing that needs cooking. You will not want to.

Organiser's rules

  • Condition your cardio beforehand.
  • Waterproof everything — clothes, gadgets, valuables, in ziplocks.
  • Pack an extra layer and a headlamp. Conditions change.
  • Bring your own trash bag. Kung anong inakyat, iyon din ang ibababa — down to the smallest candy wrapper.
  • Never rely entirely on trail water.
  • Buddy system. Nobody separates from the group, start to finish.
