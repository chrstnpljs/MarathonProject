Reading the schedule

  • Both days are roughly 7–9 hours of trekking, not the 10–14 in the general brief. Kinder than expected, but 32 km and 2,650 m of gain still land somewhere.
  • Day 1 finishes at 2:30 PM. That afternoon at Bagu is the real reward — falls, rest, and the social. Save something for it.
  • Day 2's swim comes at 11:00 AM with five more hours of trekking after. Plan for that, not against it.
  • Sitio Lamew at 2:00 PM is your only store. Everything before it, you carry.

Carry 1.5–2 L, not 3 L

You'll sweat roughly half a litre an hour in Bakun humidity under a pack. Across an 8-hour day that's more than anyone carries. Three litres is three kilos and still runs dry by afternoon.

Carry less and refill instead — this route crosses rivers and falls all day. A squeeze filter costs about 100 g and saves well over a kilo. Confirm the reliable sources with your guide before you commit to a light load.

Where to fill

  • Fast-moving water only. Skip standing pools and anything warm.
  • Collect above sitios, rice terraces and vegetable gardens. Benguet farms sit right on these watersheds.
  • A filter handles bacteria and protozoa, not pesticide or fertiliser runoff. Where you collect matters more than the filter itself.
  • Falls and side tributaries beat the main river.

The numbers

An 8-hour day at this gain burns somewhere around 3,500–4,500 kcal. You won't eat that, and shouldn't try. Cover roughly half while moving and accept the deficit.

  • 200–250 kcal per hour on the move
  • 40–50 g carbs per hour — the number that actually matters
  • ~500 mg sodium per hour in this heat
  • Eat every 45–60 minutes, on a timer, from the first hour

The commonest mistake on a day this long is not eating early because you feel fine. By the time you feel bad you're hours behind and can't catch up mid-climb.

Day 1

6:00 AM · Sitio Caang
Breakfast — load carbs

Rice and whatever protein is served. Go easy on anything heavily fried; fat sits in your stomach for hours.

7:00 AM
First snack, wanted or not

Something simple and sweet. This sets the habit for the rest of the day.

Every 45–60 min after
Alternate sweet and salty

Rotating flavours keeps you eating. Flavour fatigue is the real reason people stop. Gel, then crackers, then trail mix, then chocolate.

12:00 PM · on the trail
Lunch — half now, half later

Eat half, walk 30 minutes, finish it. A full stomach on a climb pulls blood away from your legs.

1:30 PM
Last push — fast sugar

Gel or candy. Your gut is tired and won't handle anything complicated.

2:30 – 3:00 PM · Bagu
Recovery window

Carbs and protein within 30 minutes of arriving, while your muscles still absorb well. Eat something even though dinner is at 6:00 and you're not hungry. Day 2 is decided here.

6:00 PM
Early dinner

Eat properly. Then a small top-up before lights off — you're still repairing overnight in the cold.

Notes

  • Chocnut melts into paste by noon. Bag it separately or accept the mess.
  • Bananas and kakanin are excellent and heavy. One piece, not four.
  • Repack out of boxes into ziplocks — saves weight and gives you your trash bags.
  • Nothing that needs cooking. You won't want to.

Organiser's rules

  • Condition your cardio beforehand.
  • Waterproof everything — clothes, gadgets, valuables, in ziplocks.
  • Pack an extra layer and a headlamp. Conditions change.
  • Bring your own trash bag. Kung anong inakyat, iyon din ang ibababa — down to the smallest candy wrapper.
  • Never rely entirely on trail water.
  • Buddy system. Nobody separates from the group, start to finish.

At the falls

  • Nobody swims alone, and nobody swims out of sight of the group.
  • Mountain water is colder than it looks and cramps come fast on tired legs. Get in gradually.
  • Don't jump from anything you haven't seen someone else clear first — depth changes with rainfall.
  • Currents run strongest after rain. If the guide says no, it's no.
  • Change out of wet clothes before moving on. Wet fabric on a windy ridge chills you fast.
